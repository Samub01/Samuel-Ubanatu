Who's a CODA

If one or both of your parents are Deaf, you're a CODA.

It doesn't matter whether you sign fluently, a little, or not at all, or whether you are hearing or deaf yourself. Most CODAs are hearing, and many grew up interpreting phone calls, doctor's appointments and parents' evenings long before they could spell "interpreter".

That experience shapes people. CODAs often say they feel at home in both Deaf and hearing spaces, yet fully part of neither. Meeting others who just get it makes a real difference.

  • CODAChild of Deaf Adult(s)Anyone, of any age, with at least one Deaf parent.
  • KODAKid of Deaf Adult(s)Used for younger CODAs, usually under 18.
  • BSLBritish Sign LanguageThe sign language of Deaf communities in the UK.
  • ISLIrish Sign LanguageThe sign language of Deaf communities in Ireland.

What we do

Contact, peer support and a heritage worth celebrating

CODA UK & Ireland was set up in 2011 so CODAs would have somewhere to find each other. Everything we run grows out of that.

Peer support

People who understand

Talk with other CODAs about family, language brokering, identity and the things that are hard to explain to anyone who didn't grow up with them.

Social

Meet-ups for every age

Activities for KODAs, teenagers and adult CODAs, so nobody has to wait until adulthood to find their people.

Heritage

Celebrating Deaf family culture

Being raised in a Deaf family is a cultural inheritance. We mark it, share it, and make space for CODAs to be proud of it.

Research

Adding CODA voices to the evidence

We work with researchers, including University College Cork's study CODA: A Hidden Minority Amongst the Majority, funded by Research Ireland.

CODA Festival

Two days of CODAs in one place

In 2024 Ireland held its first national CODA Festival at University College Cork: talks, workshops and plenty of time simply to be together.
