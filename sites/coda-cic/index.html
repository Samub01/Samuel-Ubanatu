Why CODA exists

The organisations closest to communities have the least support to govern AI well

Large firms have compliance teams. A food bank, a housing association or a local health project usually has a willing manager and a busy week.

AI is arriving through the back door

It comes built into the tools people already use: case management, recruitment, chatbots and analytics. Often nobody decided to adopt it.

The rules are real but hard to read

UK GDPR, the Equality Act and ICO guidance all apply. Turning them into everyday practice takes time small teams don't have.

The people affected are rarely asked

Decisions about data and automated systems get made without the service users, staff and residents who live with the results.

What we do

Practical governance, sized for the organisation in front of us

We prioritise organisations that could not otherwise afford this kind of support.

Review

Data and AI health checks

A plain-English review of the data you hold and the automated tools you use, with a short list of what to fix first.

  • Data and system inventory
  • Risk register you can keep up to date
  • Quick wins and longer-term actions

Assess

Impact assessments and transparency records

Help completing DPIAs, equality impact assessments and algorithmic transparency records before a system goes live.

  • Data protection impact assessments
  • Bias and fairness checks
  • Public-facing transparency records

Train

Data and AI literacy

Sessions for staff, trustees and volunteers on using AI tools safely, spotting risk and asking suppliers the right questions.

  • Trustee and board briefings
  • Safe use of generative AI at work
  • Supplier due-diligence questions

Involve

Community oversight panels

We set up and facilitate panels of service users and residents who review how data and AI are used, and whose views are reported back to the organisation.

  • Panel recruitment and facilitation
  • Accessible briefing materials
  • Published outcomes

How we work

From first conversation to public report

  1. Listen

    We start with what the organisation is trying to achieve and who it serves.

  2. Map

    We list the data held, the systems in use and where automated decisions touch people.

  3. Assess

    We weigh each risk against UK GDPR, the Equality Act and ICO guidance, in proportion to its size.

  4. Act and report back

    We agree fixes, then share what changed with the community it affects.

Our principles

What we hold ourselves and our partners to

  • TransparencyPeople can find out when data or AI is used about them, and why.
  • AccountabilityA named person answers for every system and every decision.
  • FairnessOutcomes are checked across groups, not only on average.
  • PrivacyCollect the least data needed and keep it no longer than necessary.
  • ParticipationThe people affected help shape the rules, not only receive them.
  • ProportionalityGovernance fits the risk. A newsletter tool isn't a benefits algorithm.

Why a community interest company

Built so the mission can't be sold off

A community interest company is a UK limited company set up for community benefit rather than private profit. We chose this form because oversight only works if the overseer is independent.

CODA is limited by guarantee and has no shareholders. Our registration with Companies House and the CIC Regulator is in progress.

  • 01Community interest testWe must show each year, in a public report, how our work benefits the community.
  • 02Asset lockOur assets and surpluses stay with community purposes and cannot be paid out to owners.
  • 03Surpluses reinvestedIncome from paid work funds free and low-cost support for smaller groups.
  • 04Independent regulatorCICs are overseen by the Office of the Regulator of Community Interest Companies.
